# Final Presentation Outline

## Presentation format

- Working title: **The Decision Trail**
- Demonstration project: **Upgrade With Confidence**
- Format: guided case-study webpage with 11 presentation chapters
- Recommended speaking time: 9–10 minutes, followed by questions
- Audience: AI class reviewers evaluating the system, process, design response, evidence discipline, and responsible use of AI
- Visual direction: black-and-white editorial system, large geometric type, generous whitespace, readable product screens, and restrained motion

## Story spine

The submission follows one argument:

> AI-assisted UX work becomes trustworthy when the path from evidence to decision remains visible.

The story moves through three acts:

1. **The trust gap** — AI can accelerate output while hiding reasoning.
2. **The system in action** — the Orchestrator carries one difficult upgrade question through six governed phases.
3. **The accountable outcome** — the prototype changes under validation, the support layer stays within its boundaries, and the final evidence limits remain explicit.

## Chapter 1 — Opening

**Headline:** From a vague request to a decision you can defend.

Introduce the UX Orchestrator and the synthetic Upgrade With Confidence project as one connected submission. Establish the customer moment immediately:

> “Before I place this order, what exactly changes?”

**Visual:** Hero statement plus live project-state card.

## Chapter 2 — The trust gap

**Headline:** AI can make UX work faster. It can also make the reasoning disappear.

Explain the three protections built into the system:

- start from approved inputs;
- give every AI agent a bounded responsibility;
- keep consequential decisions human.

## Chapter 3 — The six-phase system

**Headline:** Six phases. One question at every gate: do we know enough to move forward?

Walk through Intake, Discover, Define, Design, Validate, and Deliver. For each phase show:

`Inputs → Agent → Skills → Artifacts → Human gate → Recorded decision`

Emphasize **what changed because of the phase**, not only what it produced.

## Chapter 4 — The question

**Headline:** A framework matters only when it can carry a messy decision from ambiguity to action.

Show why a device upgrade is not one price. The design must help someone distinguish what is due today, what appears on the next bill, what changes every month, and which conditions could still alter the amount.

**Disclosure:** Transaction complexity is supported by public evidence. Customer confusion and conversion impact remain hypotheses.

## Chapter 5 — The decision trail

**Headline:** One customer question shaped every decision that followed.

Read the traceability chain in plain language before showing artifact IDs:

`Verified complexity → Chosen risk → Desired outcome → Upgrade Impact → Bounded validation`

## Chapter 6 — Where confidence breaks

**Headline:** The redesign begins where confidence breaks.

Use the large staged comparison:

1. **Current** — the journey supports shopping but distributes financial consequences.
2. **Audit** — observable design gaps are recorded without calling them customer findings.
3. **Proposed** — one persistent Upgrade Impact view connects immediate, recurring, delayed, and conditional effects.

## Chapter 7 — The design response

**Headline:** A decision aid—not another checkout summary.

Demonstrate the six-step prototype: eligible line, device and payment, trade-in, Upgrade Impact, final review, and confirmation. Call out three behaviors: understand before commitment, edit without losing context, and recover without duplicating an order.

## Chapter 8 — The prototype pushed back

**Headline:** The first dry run exposed where the design was telling the wrong story.

Show the two revisions: values appeared before their causes, and recovery offered action before order status was known. Explain that the project returned to Design and preserved both the failed observation and separate retest.

## Chapter 9 — Questions outside the flow

**Headline:** The transaction handles choices. Upgrade Guide handles questions.

Position Upgrade Guide accurately:

- It floats above the case study as an optional contextual layer, so a question does not interrupt the decision story.

- a separate public-information proof of concept;
- grounded in a verified 150-question Verizon FAQ set;
- useful for general upgrade guidance;
- unable to access an account, bill, order, private eligibility status, or live inventory.

It complements the synthetic Northstar transaction; it is not embedded account intelligence.

## Chapter 10 — The evidence boundary

**Headline:** A credible case study says where its evidence ends.

**Supported:** orchestration, traceability, executable prototype behavior, recorded revision, targeted retest, and FAQ response boundaries.

**Not supported:** real customer usability, conversion lift, production feasibility, WCAG conformance, or live account integration.

## Chapter 11 — What the trail produced

**Headline:** The result is not only a redesigned upgrade flow. It is a visible account of how the decision changed.

Close with three outcomes: reasoning stayed connected, the prototype was allowed to fail and improve, and confidence remained a hypothesis ready for real research. Keep the deliverable inventory secondary and expandable.

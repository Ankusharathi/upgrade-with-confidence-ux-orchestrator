# Define Information Architecture

## Goal

Make the information model understandable and governable so users can find, distinguish, and act on the right objects within their role and context.

## Method

1. Declare whether the artifact documents current IA, proposed IA, or a hypothesis.
2. Inventory core information objects, attributes, relationships, identifiers, lifecycle states, ownership, sensitivity, and permission rules.
3. Define taxonomy, hierarchy, labels, synonyms, navigation paths, search and filter needs, and cross-links.
4. Map information requirements to roles, tasks, journey stages, channels, and consequential decisions.
5. Check ambiguity, duplicate concepts, overloaded labels, hidden dependencies, depth, findability, privacy exposure, and accessibility of structure.
6. Record content governance, source of truth, ownership, localization, versioning, and validation needs.

## Rules

- Do not treat a sitemap as the complete IA.
- Do not validate labels through expert preference alone.
- Do not expose sensitive identifiers merely to improve recognition.
- Distinguish conceptual information structure from screen layout and technical data schema.
- Proposed structures require validation with representative tasks and users.

## Output

Create `../templates/INFORMATION-ARCHITECTURE.md` with objects, taxonomy, hierarchy, navigation, permissions, governance, evidence, assumptions, and validation needs.
